Shape the Line, Not the Paragraph

Drawing text line by line from a certified width model: the same pixels, exact positions, and a cost that follows what changes

Raouf Rahiche · Version 1.0 · {{date}} · github.com/Rahiche/break-before-you-shape
A companion to Break Before You Shape (paper/paper.html). Every number here is read from result files produced on one {{cpu}} machine and an Android emulator on it.

Abstract. Flutter shapes a paragraph as one long line, cuts that line into lines, and paints every line. A certified per-font width model (the sidecar of the companion paper) gives Flutter's exact line breaks before anything is shaped. We test what that allows at drawing time: laying out and drawing each line as its own one-line paragraph, at the position the model gives. In Flutter {{flutter_version}} on macOS, drawing line by line gave the same pixels as Flutter's own paragraph in {{px_identical:,}} of {{px_cases:,}} comparisons ({{px_share:.1f}}%), and at 16 px in every comparison under all three renderers we tried (Impeller and Skia on macOS, Impeller on an Android emulator). Where they differ, the paragraph is the less exact of the two: at 18.4 px a line drawn alone is within {{drift_18_4_maxAloneErrPx:.5f}} px of the exact positions, the same line in its paragraph up to {{drift_18_4_maxWholeErrPx:.4f}} px away. In long text the paragraph's coordinates move line breaks: in a {{long_200k_chars:,}}-character text Flutter breaks {{long_200k_paragraphsWhereTheWholeTextBreaksDifferently}} paragraphs differently from the same paragraphs laid out alone, and past a million pixels along its shaped line it starts new lines up to {{long_short_max:.1f}} px before a {{long_width:.0f}} px box is full. Drawn line by line, all {{long_paragraphs_all}} paragraphs match Flutter's layout of the paragraph alone. The cost then follows what changes: a streamed answer costs {{stream_line_late_min:.0f}}–{{stream_line_late_max:.0f}} µs per token at its end, from {{s500_chars:,.0f}} to {{s8000_chars:,.0f}} characters, where one Text costs {{s8000_wholeText_late_us:,.0f}} µs per token at {{s8000_chars:,.0f}}; and the first frame of a {{long_200k_chars:,}}-character text takes {{long_200k_firstFrameLineByLineMs:.1f}} ms instead of {{long_200k_firstFrameWholeMs:.1f}} ms.

1 · Introduction

Read in its source (the companion repository's research/ENGINE.md, §2), Flutter's text engine, SkParagraph, does three things in order. ParagraphImpl::layout shapes the whole paragraph as one endless line (shapeTextIntoEndlessLine). breakShapedTextIntoLines then cuts that line into lines by summing cluster widths. ParagraphImpl::paint paints every line, whether it is on screen or not. Three consequences follow, and they are the subject of this paper.

  • Cost follows the paragraph, not the change. Any change to the text makes a new paragraph, and Flutter's paragraph cache is keyed by the whole text: a streamed answer is shaped again, whole, at every token.
  • Cost follows the paragraph, not the screen. A long text is shaped and laid out in full before its first frame, and every line is painted.
  • Positions come from one long line. A line's glyphs are placed from the paragraph-wide shaping, sliced where the line starts. Skia's coordinates are float32 (SkScalar), which have coarse steps far along a long paragraph; §5–6 measure what that does.

The companion paper builds a per-font width model, distilled from Flutter itself and certified from the font's tables, that gives Flutter's line breaks and heights before any shaping. Once the breaks are known, nothing forces the engine to shape a paragraph as one line: each line can be laid out as its own one-line paragraph and drawn where the model says. This paper measures what that changes.

Contributions.

  1. Line-by-line drawing for Flutter, from the sidecar's breaks: per-paragraph hand-backs, a streaming variant that re-breaks only the end of a growing text, and a long-text variant that lays out only the viewport.
  2. The same pixels. A byte-for-byte comparison with Flutter's own paragraph across three renderers, four sizes, three alignments and three pixel ratios, with a control that proves the comparison can see a 1/8 px shift.
  3. A precision finding. Where the two differ, the paragraph is the one that is off: its glyph positions drift with their distance along the shaped line, and in long text Flutter's own line breaks degrade with it, to lines started tens of pixels early.
  4. Costs. Streaming and long-text timings against one Text and against one Text per paragraph.

2 · Drawing line by line

2.1 · Lines, and what goes back to Flutter

For a text, a font size and a width, the sidecar returns the start of every line, or hands the text back when it cannot be sure (a word as wide as the line, a fit decided within float error, a character it does not certify). A long text is split at its hard breaks, and each paragraph is decided on its own, with its hard break kept: on Apple platforms the character before a hard break kerns with it (measured in the companion paper). A paragraph handed back is laid out by Flutter as usual, and only that paragraph.

2.2 · Drawing

Line i's text runs from its start to the next line's start, with its trailing spaces and without the hard break that ends it. It is laid out by Flutter as a paragraph of its own, in the same style, width and alignment, and painted at y = i × h, where h is the model's line height (Flutter's heights are lines × h for one font: measured, companion paper §4). The line alone is shaped from position 0. For certified text the companion paper shows a line's width is the sum of its characters' advances and in-line pair adjustments, so shaping it alone gives it the same advances; §4 checks the pixels.

2.3 · Streaming

A streamed answer grows at its end. Under greedy breaking, appending text can only move the breaks of the last lines: a line ends at the last opportunity that fits, and every earlier line's fit is decided by words that are already complete. The streaming variant therefore re-breaks the text from the start of its second-to-last line at each token (records for that tail only, then the greedy pass), lays out the lines whose text changed, and keeps every line before the second-to-last as final. Final lines are recorded into a picture, 16 at a time, so a token paints the picture and the last lines. If the sidecar hands the tail back, Flutter lays out the whole text, as it does today. §7.1 checks this against Flutter at every token.

2.4 · Long text

For a long text the sidecar gives every line start and the exact total height (arithmetic over the width prefix, plus Flutter's layout of any paragraph handed back), and only the lines in the viewport are laid out and painted. A scroll lays out the lines that come into view.

3 · Setup

Machine. {{cpu}}, {{os}}; Flutter {{flutter_version}}; release builds. Android: an emulator ({{android_emulator}}) on the same machine; there is no phone in this study.

Renderers. macOS with Impeller on Metal (Flutter's default here), macOS with Skia on Metal (Impeller turned off in the built app), and the Android emulator with Impeller on OpenGL ES. Each run logs the backend it used.

Texts. The companion paper's corpora, built from this machine's files: Python docstrings, man pages, licence texts, and macOS interface strings in English, German, French, Russian, Ukrainian and Greek. Roboto Regular, with the companion paper's sidecar for it.

Pixels. For each text, size (px), width (150, 240 and 330 px) and configuration (start, centre and end alignment at 1×, start alignment at 2× and 3×), the paragraph and its lines are each drawn into a picture, rasterized with Picture.toImage, and compared byte for byte (RGBA). Control: in every start-aligned 1× case the lines are drawn again 1/8 px to the right, and that image must differ from the paragraph's.

Timing. The UI thread's work: laying out, and recording the paint into a picture. Rasterization runs on another thread and is not measured. Medians of 5 passes (streaming) or 7 (long text); every timed layout misses Flutter's paragraph cache.

The pixel differences have the same geography (Table 2). At 18.4 px they occur at every distance. At 14 px every differing comparison's first differing line starts beyond 4,096 px along the shaped line, at 12 px beyond 8,192 px, and at 16 px there are none. At 12 and 14 px the reported positions are exact in both layouts, so those differences arise when the glyphs are drawn far along the line; we have not traced them inside the renderers. The pattern is consistent with float32 coordinates measured from the start of the shaped line: a float32 near x moves in steps of 2⌊log₂ x⌋ − 23, 1/2048 px beyond 4,096 px and 1/1024 px beyond 8,192 px, and at 16 px Roboto's font units scale by 2−7 px, a power of two. A line drawn alone has its coordinates start at 0 and never goes far.

A Text holding a long document therefore wraps a paragraph one way, and the same paragraph shown on its own another, and the error grows with the length of the document. Drawn line by line, every paragraph is laid out from its own start, and its breaks do not depend on what precedes it.

8 · Related work

Lazy text layout is common, with estimated sizes: TextKit 2 lays out the viewport and estimates the rest, and Chrome's content-visibility skips layout of off-screen content and uses a placeholder size (research/ENGINE.md, §4–5). Line by line is lazy with exact sizes, because the breaks and heights come from a certified model rather than an estimate. Android's Minikin measures text per word through a cache and splits words at spaces, so it never shapes across a line end; SkParagraph shapes the paragraph as one line and never reshapes at line ends (§2 of the same note). Streaming chat interfaces commonly render Markdown, one widget per block, which is the per-paragraph baseline of §7.1.

9 · Limitations

  • One font, one direction. Roboto Regular, left to right. Text the sidecar does not certify (other fonts as fallback, complex scripts, emoji sequences) is handed back, and drawn by Flutter as today.
  • Not a widget yet. The measurements use dart:ui paragraphs directly. A widget would also need selection across lines, caret and hit testing, a single semantics node for screen readers, and justification (a one-line paragraph is its own last line, so TextAlign.justify does not stretch it); none is implemented. Ellipsis is not covered.
  • UI thread only. Raster time, GPU memory and memory for many small paragraphs are not measured.
  • No phone. Android results come from an emulator; timings are from the Mac.
  • Corpora. Machine-generated from local files; streaming answers are corpus paragraphs, not model output, and tokens are approximated.
  • Float rounding is engine behaviour. §5–6 describe SkParagraph in Flutter {{flutter_version}}; another version that keeps positions relative to each line would remove the differences, and with them §6's effect.
